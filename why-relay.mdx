This page covers what Relay gives a person who uses many agents, and what an app
per agent, one general assistant, or a bot on someone else's platform costs
instead.

## One inbox, many agents

The useful agents in a person's life come from many products: coding, research,
scheduling, fitness, finance. Each can have a different operator, capabilities,
data, price, and trust rules, and adding one today usually means another app,
account, subscription, notification system, permission model, and separate
history.

| Approach | Cost |
| --- | --- |
| An app per agent | Repeated installs, accounts, notifications, and disconnected history |
| One general assistant | Specialization becomes a hidden model choice or tool call; the user may not know who is acting |
| Automatic routing | The router can pick the wrong expert, and operator identity disappears |

In Relay the user adds each agent deliberately, and each agent keeps its own
identity and specialization.

## Existing channels own the relationship

Putting an agent into Telegram, Discord, Slack, WhatsApp, SMS, RCS, or iMessage
removes an app install, and can be the right answer for reach. The host platform
then defines the account, bot identity, invocation rules, history, permissions,
rich output, delivery, discovery, payments, and enforcement.

| Path | Tradeoff |
| --- | --- |
| Official bot and business APIs | Supported, but constrained by the host's contract |
| Unofficial personal-account automation | May depend on private interfaces, a linked session, or signed-in hardware. Enforcement or a platform change can break delivery |
| Carrier messaging | Real reach, plus consent and opt-out handling, sender setup, filtering, variable delivery, segmentation, and usage-based cost |

[Compare the alternatives side by side](/alternatives).

## Agent states get their own grammar

Agent work can take time, wait for approval, stream progress, return structured
output, share media, fail, and retry. Other channels compress those states into
typing indicators, message edits, bot commands, or per-channel cards.

Relay carries them directly, with one stored transcript per conversation,
explicit delivery and recovery, typed message parts, and permissioned native
capabilities.

## What Relay changes

<Columns cols={2}>
  <Card title="For the user" icon="user">
    Open a share link or reviewed profile, see who operates the agent, and add
    it explicitly instead of granting silent inbox access. Working, approval,
    failure, retry, and completion states stay visible; notifications, memory,
    media, location, payments, calls, groups, and cross-agent context stay under
    per-agent control as they ship.
  </Card>
  <Card title="For the developer" icon="code">
    Relay runs identity, profiles, conversations, ordering, delivery, sync,
    notifications, media transport, installation, safety, and the native client.
    You keep models, prompts, tools, behavior, external memory, hosting, and
    support, and connect an existing backend through a versioned REST API and
    signed webhooks with no Relay runtime or SDK.
  </Card>
</Columns>
